## Problem

The law already recognizes DAOs, as general partnerships. What it does not offer is a way to operate as an entity without handing control to a few.

A few states have enacted statutes for decentralized entities, but the statutes differ and most states have none [VERIFY count]. Courts, the IRS, and private counterparties such as insurers do not consistently honor the forms that exist. Without a recognized form, courts fall back on default law. In *Samuels v. Lido DAO*, a federal court held that the complaint plausibly alleged a DAO was a general partnership, and that investors who took part in its governance could be partners. Under this rule, anyone who participates in governance takes on risk.

So projects go offshore, and the Swiss foundation is a common choice [VERIFY prevalence]. It gives a project legal existence. But a small council runs it, and a Swiss government supervisor, federal or cantonal, holds the final say over its organization, its purpose, and its dissolution (Arts. 84–88 ZGB) [VERIFY]. A protocol that calls itself decentralized while operating through a Swiss foundation is, in law, controlled by a council and answerable to a Swiss supervisory authority.

**Key term.** *Entrenchment*: control held by a small group that the community cannot remove. Anti-entrenchment is the paper's test for decentralization. Community ownership and openness are supporting values.

## Thesis (v1, 2026-10-30)

> Because US law recognizes decentralized entities only through a patchwork of non-uniform state statutes that courts, the IRS, and private counterparties do not consistently honor, protocol projects cannot operate as legal entities without entrenching control. The law already recognizes DAOs, as general partnerships. What it does not offer is a way to operate as an entity without handing control to a few. Without a form, courts treat a protocol as a general partnership, and anyone who governs risks personal liability. With an offshore form such as the Swiss foundation, a small council runs the project and a Swiss government supervisor holds the final say. Congress should enact a federal recognition layer over state-law decentralized entities so that operating under law no longer requires entrenching control.
