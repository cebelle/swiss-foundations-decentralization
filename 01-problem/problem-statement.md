## Problem

US law has no consistent way to recognize decentralized entities. A few states have enacted statutes, but the statutes differ, and many states have none. Courts, the IRS, and private counterparties such as insurers do not consistently honor the forms that do exist. Without a recognized form, courts fall back on default law. In *Samuels v. Lido DAO*, a federal court held that the complaint plausibly alleged a DAO was a general partnership, and that investors who took part in its governance could be partners. The result is a choice between legal recognition and decentralization. Many projects choose recognition offshore, through structures such as the Swiss foundation, that centralize control.

## Thesis (v0, approved 2026-10-30)

> Because US law recognizes decentralized entities only through a patchwork of non-uniform state statutes that courts, the IRS, and private counterparties do not consistently honor, protocol projects must choose between legal recognition and decentralization. Many choose recognition offshore, through structures such as the Swiss foundation, that centralize control. Congress should enact a federal recognition layer over state-law decentralized entities so that recognition no longer requires centralization.
