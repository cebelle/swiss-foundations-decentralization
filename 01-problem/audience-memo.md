## The decision they face

Whether federal law should recognize state-law decentralized entities, and on what terms: tax classification, a liability rule that displaces default partnership law, and recognition across state lines.

## What the paper must show them

1. **Fragmentation is real.** State statutes differ, many states have none, and courts, the IRS, and insurers do not consistently honor the forms that exist.
2. **The bind.** The law already recognizes DAOs, as general partnerships. What it does not offer is a way to operate as an entity without handing control to a few. *Samuels v. Lido DAO* shows the cost of having no form: anyone who takes part in governance may be treated as a general partner.
3. **The offshore answer entrenches control.** In a Swiss foundation, a small council runs the project and a Swiss government supervisor holds the final say. The Swiss foundation is the case study.
4. **Only federal law fixes it.** The entities are borderless, and recognition by the IRS and federal courts is federal by nature.
5. **A statutory design they could draft from.**
6. **Answers to beliefs 1–4.**

## What they do not need

Swiss doctrine in depth. The paper covers Swiss law only as far as needed to show where control in a foundation actually rests.
