## Decisions (author)

1. **Primary reader:** US policymakers: elected officials, legislators, and private actors working to change the law. Swiss supervisors are not the target, because Swiss foundation law is working as designed.
2. **Aim:** give protocol entities a path to operate in the United States. This moves toward candidate C in `01-problem/candidates-v0.md`.
3. **Motivation:** the author is not on the academic market. The target reader is different from the prior article's (the ALI).
4. **Venue:** a law review, preferably a blockchain or technology law journal.
5. **Claim type:** normative reform thesis.
6. **Core problem:** state recognition of decentralized entities is not uniform, and not all states recognize them. Courts, the IRS, and private counterparties such as insurers do not consistently honor what exists.
7. **Central framing (accepted):** under current US law, protocol projects must choose between legal recognition and decentralization.
8. **Remedy:** a federal statute that creates a federal recognition layer over state-law entities, modeled on the nonprofit pattern (state formation plus federal recognition). The author chose this over a federal charter.
9. **Answer to the federalism objection:** both (a) the entities are borderless, which is what makes the problem unique, and (b) only federal law can secure recognition by the IRS and federal courts, which grounds the argument in practical terms.
10. **Anchor case:** *Samuels v. Lido DAO* will be considered as the opening for the discussion of courts.
11. **Rejected:** conditioning federal recognition "on the condition that they are actually decentralized." This phrase is out of the thesis.

## Working thesis, v0 (author-approved wording pending)

> Because US law recognizes decentralized entities only through a patchwork of non-uniform state statutes that courts, the IRS, and private counterparties do not consistently honor, protocol projects must choose between legal recognition and decentralization. Many choose recognition offshore, through structures such as the Swiss foundation, that centralize control. Congress should enact a federal recognition layer over state-law decentralized entities so that recognition no longer requires centralization.

The claim that Swiss foundations centralize control is a hypothesis to test in Phase 3.
