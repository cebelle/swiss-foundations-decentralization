## Working thesis, v0 (approved by author 2026-10-30)

> Because US law recognizes decentralized entities only through a patchwork of non-uniform state statutes that courts, the IRS, and private counterparties do not consistently honor, protocol projects must choose between legal recognition and decentralization. Many choose recognition offshore, through structures such as the Swiss foundation, that centralize control. Congress should enact a federal recognition layer over state-law decentralized entities so that recognition no longer requires centralization.

The claim that Swiss foundations centralize control is a hypothesis to test in Phase 3.

## Open questions

1. **Eligibility.** Any federal recognition statute has to define which entities qualify. That is a design question for the paper body, not the thesis.
2. **Lido and liability.** Does the remedy preserve liability for actors who actually control an entity? Lido turns on governance participation, so the paper needs an answer.
3. **Insurers.** The claim about insurers needs a source in Phase 3, or it should be cut.
4. **Spring cycle.** Confirm submission windows and word limits for the target blockchain and technology journals [VERIFY].
5. **Statutory landscape** [VERIFY]: the Wyoming DAO LLC (2021) and DUNA (2024), the Utah DAO Act (2024), any IRS guidance on DAO classification, and federal market-structure legislation as of Oct. 2026.
6. **The bind needs sharper terms.** "Legal recognition" is ambiguous: courts already recognize DAOs as general partnerships, just not on terms projects want. The author's framing is that the choice is between a decentralized ethos (community ownership, openness, resistance to entrenchment) and recognition that brings legal existence and the practical ability to operate. Wording is being workshopped, and the thesis, memo item 2, and problem statement will be updated once it is settled.
